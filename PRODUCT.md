## Platform

web

## Users

DataSolace serves UK small business owners, operators, and founder-led teams that are spending too much time on manual admin, fragmented tools, repeated handoffs, inconsistent documentation, and process work that should not depend on memory.

These visitors often need practical automation but do not want a distant agency, a generic SaaS platform, or a noisy "AI automation" pitch. The site should make the small-team advantage clear: visitors speak directly with the owners, get personal attention, and work with people who can understand messy real-world operations before proposing systems.

## Product Purpose

The website exists to establish trust in DataSolace as a capable process automation partner for small businesses. It should explain how DataSolace helps make routine work clearer, faster, safer, and easier to repeat, then convert qualified visitors into conversations or appointments.

Success looks like a visitor understanding that DataSolace can help untangle operational friction, feeling that the team is technically capable and personally approachable, and knowing the next step to start a practical conversation about their process.

## Positioning

DataSolace maps the process before choosing the tool, builds the system, and then keeps it running — with the owners doing the work throughout.

That full span is the claim a neighbouring provider cannot truthfully copy. A no-code agency will connect tools but does not run infrastructure. An IT consultancy will run infrastructure but arrives with a stack rather than starting from how the work actually happens. A SaaS platform supplies a product, not an understanding of one business's handoffs. DataSolace covers process discovery, integration, custom build, and ongoing operation as one continuous relationship, at a scale small enough that the people who designed the system are the people who answer when it needs changing.

## Operating Context

Clients are small UK businesses where the process is distributed across a handful of people and a set of tools that were adopted one at a time rather than chosen together. Work moves between inboxes, forms, spreadsheets, scheduling, and record-keeping, with the joins between them handled manually and held in individual memory.

Engagements begin with a direct conversation rather than a specification. The process is observed and documented before any system is proposed. Delivered systems are then maintained by DataSolace rather than handed over and abandoned, so the operating relationship is ongoing rather than project-terminal.

Owners and operators evaluate the site themselves, usually alongside running the business rather than in a dedicated procurement process.

## Capabilities and Constraints

Confirmed capabilities delivered for small business clients:

- **Process mapping and documentation** — observing how work actually runs, then writing it down so it stops depending on memory.
- **Systems integration** — connecting existing tools, APIs, and data flows so records move between systems that do not natively talk.
- **Custom internal tools** — bespoke apps, forms, dashboards, and portals built around a specific workflow.
- **Hosting and infrastructure** — running and maintaining the delivered systems, including self-hosting, backups, and ongoing support.

Constraints:

- Networking and infrastructure are retained as a **business capability**, folded into the small business offer. The residential and smart-home framing is retired from site-facing copy.
- The team is small and owner-led. Claims must not imply a scale, headcount, or service tier the business does not have.

Decided (July 2026):

- **Smart Home Index** remains on the site as compact build proof on the services page — presented as a product DataSolace designed, built, and operates, evidencing data modelling and production infrastructure capability. It is not pitched as a smart-home offer and carries no dedicated newsletter signup.
- **Self Hosted Concierge** is retired as a named service. Its substance (self-hosting, privacy-respecting setups, maintenance) is absorbed into the Hosting and Infrastructure capability.
- **The two residential case studies** (damp protection, smart-home mobility) remain published, explicitly framed as work from the earlier residential systems practice — the engineering discipline behind the small business offer, not part of it. Internal business engagements stay confidential, and the portfolio page says so.
- **Client websites are featured by name with consent.** The portfolio leads with commissioned public websites (BarkBuddiez, Cara's Kitchen) as live, verifiable client work, each walked through as the front door of a business process rather than as web design for its own sake.

## Brand Commitments

Brand line: **DataSolace turns everyday complexity into calm, capable systems.**

Brand personality: clear, assured, capable.

Clear means automation people can understand. Copy, proposals, interfaces, and explanations should make the work feel legible without hiding important detail.

Assured means calm expertise. DataSolace should feel dependable, technically fluent, and in control without becoming cold, corporate, or intimidating.

Capable means practical implementation. The brand should show that DataSolace builds and maintains useful systems that handle real operational details, not just advice or abstract automation ideas.

The voice should feel direct, thoughtful, and owner-led. DataSolace should come across as technically fluent without sounding like a corporate IT consultancy, boutique without sounding precious, and personal without becoming informal, chatty, or guru-like.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic SaaS marketing: vague automation claims, inflated productivity language, abstract diagrams, template hero sections, and buttons that feel detached from the visitor's real operational problem.

Avoid "AI automation guru" energy: hype about replacing staff, magic workflows, prompt-driven spectacle, hustle language, and shallow claims about saving time without understanding the business.

Avoid no-code agency gloss: walls of app logos, Zapier-like connector diagrams, generic funnel language, and a sense that every problem can be solved with the same tool stack.

Avoid corporate IT consultancy tone: faceless enterprise language, committee-written copy, stock business imagery, and claims that feel larger than the team or service model.

Avoid bookkeeping-software dullness: flat administrative visuals, bland office stock photography, and copy that makes process improvement feel like compliance paperwork rather than relief.

## Evidence on Hand

**What exists.**

- **Delivered small business work.** Real process automation has been delivered for small business clients. Internal-systems work is **anonymised only**: it can be described concretely in terms of the situation and what changed, but clients cannot be named and the work cannot be visually identified. No client photographs, screenshots, or recognisable artefacts from internal systems may appear on the site.
- **Commissioned public client websites** are exempt from the anonymity rule where the client has consented to being featured. Two exist and are published as named, linked case studies (consent obtained July 2026): **BarkBuddiez** (barkbuddiezgrooming.co.uk, one-to-one dog grooming, launched June 2026) and **Cara's Kitchen** (caras.kitchen, handmade preserves, launched spring 2026). Both are built, self-hosted, and maintained by DataSolace on the same stack as its own systems.
- **Own internal systems.** DataSolace runs its own automations and tooling. These are showable and are the only operational systems that can be depicted directly.
- **Adjacent infrastructure and networking work.** Previously delivered under the older positioning, this involved genuine systems and process work and legitimately reframes as business capability.
- **Smart Home Index** — a real, live, free device comparison platform at `smarthomeindex.com`, with existing screenshots (`public/SHI-*.webp`). Real proof of build capability, but residential in subject.
- **Existing site imagery**, all first-party: `public/business_documentation.webp`, `public/customer_feedback.webp`, `public/networking-and-infrastructure.webp`, `public/hands_on_a_window.webp`, `public/lightbulbs.webp`, `public/home-automation.webp`, `public/self-hosted-concierge.webp`, `public/logo.webp`.

**Absences that must not be fabricated.**

- **No named clients.** No client names, logos, or identifiable details exist for use.
- **No testimonials or quotes.** `customer_feedback.webp` is an image asset, not a collection of real published testimonials.
- **No published internal-systems case study for a small business.** The client website case studies demonstrate public-facing work; the process automation delivered inside businesses remains describable only in anonymised terms.
- **No metrics, benchmarks, time-saved figures, client counts, or years-in-business claims** have been established. These must not be invented to fill a proof section.
- **No photography of client premises, staff, or real operations** is available or permitted.

**Consequence for design.** Proof on this site must be carried by specificity of description, anonymised scenarios, first-party imagery, and DataSolace's own systems — not by named logos, client photography, testimonials, or numbers. Any visual implying a real identifiable customer would be a fabrication.

## Product Principles

1. **Start with the process before the tool.** Demonstrate understanding of the workflow, people, exceptions, and risks before proposing any system.
2. **Make the owner-led advantage visible.** Visitors should feel they can talk to real decision-makers who will understand the process and stay close to the work.
3. **Lead with practical confidence.** Show what can be made simpler, safer, or easier to run using specific operational outcomes rather than broad claims.
4. **Balance technical depth with plain language.** Convey serious systems expertise without requiring the reader to parse engineering jargon.
5. **Make messy work feel legible.** Unclear handoffs, repeated admin, scattered information, and fragile routines should feel mappable, improvable, and maintainable.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target strong practical accessibility, with WCAG AA as the working baseline for future UI decisions. Prioritize readable type, high contrast, clear focus states, touch-friendly controls, and forms that are easy to complete.

Respect reduced-motion preferences. Motion can support polish and orientation, but it should never be required to understand or use the site.

Design with older users in mind. Keep navigation predictable, copy concrete, interaction targets generous, and avoid low-contrast placeholder text or visually subtle form states.
